Overview: 
PUSHPAK Grand Challenge 2026: Towards Drone Excellence 
A National Innovation Challenge under the PUSHPAK: National Mission on Drone 
Technology. 
Funded by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology 
(MeitY), Government of India. 
Hosted by IIT Bombay. 
In collaboration with  VJTI Mumbai – Veermata Jijabai Technological 
Institute, Maharashtra (VJTI). 
India is entering a new era of autonomous aerial systems, intelligent mobility, 
and drone-enabled digital infrastructure. The PUSHPAK Grand Challenge 2026 is 
a national platform created to identify and accelerate high-impact engineering 
solutions that can address real-world problems through drones, AI, robotics, 
sensing, communication systems, and autonomous technologies. 
This is not a conventional hackathon or idea competition. The Grand Challenge is 
designed to take promising technical concepts through evaluation, mentoring, 
prototyping, testing, and deployment-oriented development. Selected teams will 
work with researchers, domain experts, and industry mentors to transform 
innovative ideas into functional, field-ready prototypes.

Objective 1: Drone Forensics Toolkit Development 
1.1 Objective 
The objective of this Grand Challenge is to design, develop, and validate an 
indigenous Drone Forensic Toolkit capable of acquiring, preserving, analyzing, 
and reporting digital evidence from Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) and their 
associated components in a forensically sound manner. The toolkit should 
support evidence acquisition from onboard storage, flight controllers, remote 
 


=== PAGE 5 ===
 
controllers, removable media, companion mobile applications, and other relevant 
data sources while ensuring integrity through cryptographic hashing. The 
solution should enable automated data extraction, timeline reconstruction, flight 
path analysis, metadata extraction, and comprehensive forensic reporting, and 
should be scalable, extensible, and suitable for deployment by law enforcement 
agencies, forensic laboratories, regulatory authorities, and defence organizations 
for investigation of drone-related incidents. 
1.2 Problem Statement 
 
Despite the growing importance of drone forensics, the investigation process 
remains challenging due to diverse hardware architectures, proprietary data 
formats, encrypted storage, vendor-specific communication protocols, volatile 
memory, and the absence of standardized forensic acquisition and analysis 
methodologies. These challenges make it difficult to preserve evidence integrity 
and perform comprehensive forensic investigations across different UAV 
platforms. 
The toolkit should support the acquisition of digital evidence from onboard 
storage, removable storage media, flight controllers, remote controllers. The 
proposed solution should incorporate secure evidence preservation mechanisms, 
cryptographic hashing for integrity verification, chain-of-custody management, 
automated evidence parsing, timeline reconstruction, and standardized forensic 
reporting suitable for investigative and legal proceedings. The toolkit should also 
demonstrate repeatable and verifiable forensic acquisition processes under 
controlled test conditions. 
Drone Forensic Toolkit system should be modular, scalable, vendor-independent, 
and easily extensible to support emerging UAV technologies, new drone 
platforms, and evolving forensic requirements with minimal modifications. The 
ultimate goal is to establish an indigenous, reliable, and standardized drone 
forensic capability that can support law enforcement agencies, regulatory bodies, 
forensic laboratories, defense organizations, and cybersecurity professionals in 
conducting efficient and legally defensible drone investigations.

Objective 2 Drone Intrusion Detection System (Drone IDS) 
2.1 Objective 
The objective of this Grand Challenge is to encourage the development of 
innovative, indigenous, and deployable Drone Intrusion Detection Systems 
(Drone IDS) that provide real-time cyber threat detection and monitoring for 
UAV platforms. The challenge aims to foster solutions capable of detecting 
communication, navigation, firmware, and control system attacks with high 
accuracy, low latency, and minimal resource overhead, while enabling seamless 
integration into existing and future UAV platforms for civilian, industrial, and 
defence applications. 
2.2 Problem Statement 
The objective of this challenge is to design, develop, and validate an indigenous 
Drone Intrusion Detection System (Drone IDS) capable of continuously 
monitoring UAV systems and detecting cyber threats in real time. The Drone IDS 
should operate autonomously with minimal computational and power overhead, 
making it suitable for deployment on resource-constrained UAV platforms. It 
should provide real-time threat detection, event logging, attack classification, 
secure alert generation, and comprehensive reporting to support timely response 
and post-incident analysis. The solution should be modular, scalable, and 
interoperable with a wide range of UAV platforms and communication protocols, 
enabling seamless integration into existing and future indigenous drone 
ecosystems. 
The Drone IDS system should demonstrate reliable detection of representative 
cyber threats under controlled test conditions and be suitable for future 
deployment in civilian, industrial, critical infrastructure, public safety, and 
defence applications. The challenge focuses on developing an onboard cyber 
defence capability that enhances the resilience, safety, security, and 
trustworthiness of next-generation UAV platforms.
