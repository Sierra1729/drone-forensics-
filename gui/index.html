Altitude Profile & Multi-Sensor Cross-Validation
Fused Altitude (True) Barometer (Air Pressure) GPS Altitude (Satellites)
📖 What this shows: How high the drone flew across the mission duration.
🎯 Normal behavior: Smooth ascent to cruising altitude and steady descent to landing. The blue (fused), green (air pressure), and yellow (GPS) lines should closely trace each other.
🔍 What to look for in court: A sudden vertical drop to zero indicates a mid-air collision, motor cut, or ground impact. Divergence between lines indicates sensor tampering or barometer failure.
Attitude Angles & Vehicle Orientation (Euler Kinematics)
Roll (Bank Left/Right °) Pitch (Nose Up/Down °) Yaw (Compass Heading 0-360°)
📖 What this shows: The physical tilting and orientation of the drone in the sky.
🎯 Normal behavior: Roll and pitch tilt gently between 5° to 35° to steer. Yaw smoothly rotates as the drone turns.
🔍 What to look for in court: A roll or pitch exceeding 60° represents aggressive maneuvering; exceeding 90° means the drone flipped upside down or tumbled mid-air before crashing.
Velocity Vectors & Horizontal Ground Speed
Total Ground Speed (m/s) North Vx (+N / -S) East Vy (+E / -W) Vertical Vz (+Climb / -Descent)
📖 What this shows: How fast the drone was traveling horizontally and vertically.
🎯 Normal behavior: Smooth speed curves within the manufacturer's top speed rating (typically 12–25 m/s or 45–90 km/h).
🔍 What to look for in court: Rapid vertical velocity drops (Vz > -5 m/s) indicate uncontrolled freefall or crash descent.
